# Deck Types & Specialization

Guidance for tailoring presentations to specific contexts and audiences.

## Business Review Deck

**Characteristics:**
- Audience: Executives, leadership
- Length: 10-15 slides
- Tone: Data-driven, confident, solution-oriented

**Typical Flow:**
1. Title
2. Agenda
3. Executive Summary (3-4 key points)
4. Current State / Context
5. Key Metrics (dashboard)
6. Achievements / Wins
7. Challenges / Risks
8. Plan / Recommendations
9. Resource Needs
10. Next Steps

**Content Guidelines:**
- Lead with outcomes, not activities
- Use data to support every claim
- Be honest about challenges (with solutions ready)
- Keep details in appendix
- Anticipate questions and prepare backup slides

---

## Sales Deck

**Characteristics:**
- Audience: Prospective customers
- Length: 10-12 slides
- Tone: Benefit-focused, credible, urgent

**Typical Flow:**
1. Title (problem-focused headline)
2. The Problem (agitate pain point)
3. Current Solutions (and their limitations)
4. Our Solution (big picture)
5. How It Works (process)
6. Key Benefits (3-4 bullets)
7. Proof / Case Study
8. Pricing / Packages (if applicable)
9. Implementation / Next Steps
10. Call to Action

**Content Guidelines:**
- Customer-centric language ("you" not "we")
- Focus on business outcomes, not features
- Include social proof early
- Address objections proactively
- Clear, low-friction CTA

---

## Investor Pitch Deck

**Characteristics:**
- Audience: Investors, VCs
- Length: 10-12 slides (classic format)
- Tone: Visionary, data-backed, ambitious

**Typical Flow:**
1. Title (company + one-liner)
2. The Problem
3. The Solution / Product
4. Market Opportunity (TAM/SAM/SOM)
5. Business Model
6. Traction / Metrics
7. Competitive Landscape
8. Go-to-Market Strategy
9. Team
10. Financial Projections
11. The Ask (funding amount and use)
12. Closing / Contact

**Content Guidelines:**
- Show, don't just tell (product demo if possible)
- Market size must be convincing
- Traction trumps projections
- Team slide validates execution capability
- Know your numbers cold
- Keep appendix ready for deep-dive questions

---

## Training Deck

**Characteristics:**
- Audience: Employees, partners, customers
- Length: 20-40 slides (varies by topic)
- Tone: Clear, educational, engaging

**Typical Flow:**
1. Title
2. Learning Objectives
3. Why This Matters
4. Core Concept 1 (with example)
5. Practice / Exercise
6. Core Concept 2 (with example)
7. Practice / Exercise
8. Common Mistakes / Tips
9. Summary
10. Assessment / Q&A
11. Resources

**Content Guidelines:**
- One concept per slide
- Use examples from learner's context
- Build in interaction every 5-7 slides
- Include knowledge checks
- Provide reference materials
- Larger text for readability

---

## Technical Architecture Deck

**Characteristics:**
- Audience: Technical teams, architects, CTOs
- Length: 15-25 slides
- Tone: Precise, detailed, balanced

**Typical Flow:**
1. Title / Context
2. Current Architecture (and limitations)
3. Requirements / Constraints
4. Proposed Architecture (high-level)
5. Component Breakdown
6. Data Flow
7. Technology Stack
8. Security Considerations
9. Scalability Approach
10. Migration Plan
11. Risks and Mitigations
12. Timeline
13. Resources Needed
14. Decision / Approval

**Content Guidelines:**
- Diagrams are essential
- Show trade-offs explicitly
- Include alternatives considered
- Address non-functional requirements
- Cite standards or best practices
- Technical depth matched to audience

---

## Case Study Deck

**Characteristics:**
- Audience: Prospects, analysts, internal learning
- Length: 8-12 slides
- Tone: Story-driven, credible, specific

**Typical Flow:**
1. Title (client name + outcome)
2. Client Overview
3. The Challenge (before state)
4. Objectives / Goals
5. The Solution (approach + implementation)
6. Key Decisions Made
7. Results / Outcomes (metrics)
8. Client Quote / Testimonial
9. Key Learnings
10. Next Steps / Applicability

**Content Guidelines:**
- Tell a story (situation → challenge → solution → results)
- Use specific metrics, not vague claims
- Include real quotes
- Show visual before/after when possible
- Keep client confidentiality if needed
- Focus on transferable insights

---

## Strategy Review Deck

**Characteristics:**
- Audience: Leadership, board, strategy team
- Length: 12-20 slides
- Tone: Analytical, forward-looking, decisive

**Typical Flow:**
1. Title
2. Strategic Context (market changes, trends)
3. Current Position Assessment
4. Strategic Options Considered
5. Recommended Strategy
6. Strategic Pillars / Initiatives
7. Resource Implications
8. Risk Assessment
9. Success Metrics
10. Implementation Roadmap
11. Decision Points / Approvals Needed

**Content Guidelines:**
- Ground in external data (market, competition)
- Show strategic alternatives considered
- Be explicit about trade-offs
- Link to financial outcomes
- Define clear decision gates
- Include governance and accountability

---

## Project Status Deck

**Characteristics:**
- Audience: Stakeholders, project sponsors
- Length: 8-12 slides
- Tone: Transparent, action-oriented, solution-focused

**Typical Flow:**
1. Title / Project Name
2. Executive Summary (RAG status)
3. Milestones (timeline with status)
4. Accomplishments This Period
5. Upcoming Priorities
6. Issues / Risks (and mitigation)
7. Budget Status
8. Decisions Needed
9. Next Steps

**Content Guidelines:**
- Be honest about status (no green-washing)
- Focus on outcomes, not just activities
- Highlight blockers that need help
- Include photos or visuals for engagement
- Keep to a regular rhythm (weekly/bi-weekly)
- Decision items clearly called out

---

## All-Hands / Town Hall Deck

**Characteristics:**
- Audience: All employees
- Length: 15-25 slides
- Tone: Inspiring, transparent, inclusive

**Typical Flow:**
1. Title / Theme
2. Agenda
3. Company Performance Highlights
4. Key Wins (celebrate teams)
5. Strategic Priorities Update
6. People Updates (hires, milestones)
7. Department Spotlights (rotating)
8. Q&A Preview
9. Closing / Values Connection

**Content Guidelines:**
- Mix data with stories
- Celebrate people and teams
- Connect to company values/mission
- Be authentic about challenges
- Use photos of real employees
- Keep text minimal, visual heavy
